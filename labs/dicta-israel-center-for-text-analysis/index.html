About

DICTA, the Israel Center for Text Analysis, was founded by Professor Moshe Koppel of Bar-Ilan University and builds language technology for Hebrew. Its early tools served Jewish studies and digital humanities researchers, including the Nakdan vowelization tool, a Scriptural Allusion Finder and an Abbreviation Expander.

The core research team includes Shaltiel Shmidman, Avi Shmidman, who holds dual affiliations with DICTA and Bar-Ilan University, and Koppel. Its Hugging Face organization dicta-il and its GitHub organization publish models and tools for Hebrew.

Its model line began with DictaBERT in 2023 and DictaLM in the same year. DictaLM 2.0 followed in 2024 with an enlarged Hebrew vocabulary, and the DictaLM 3.0 report of February 2026 presents open 24B, 12B and 1.7B models adapted from Mistral Small 3.1, NVIDIA Nemotron Nano V2 and Qwen3, hosted under Apache 2.0.

Mission and ambitions

The center works to provide high-quality, openly available language technology for Hebrew.

Milestones

  1. 2020

    Nakdan

    Presented a professional Hebrew diacritizer at the ACL system demonstrations.

  2. 2022

    Extra-large vocabulary Hebrew BERT

    Published a contrastive analysis of Hebrew BERT models and a new model.

  3. 2023

    DictaBERT and DictaLM

    Released a BERT suite and the first generative DictaLM for Modern Hebrew.

  4. 2024

    DictaLM 2.0

    Adapted LLMs to Hebrew with enhanced vocabulary and instruction capabilities.

  5. 2026

    DictaLM 3.0

    Open Hebrew-centric 24B, 12B and 1.7B models with base and chat variants.
