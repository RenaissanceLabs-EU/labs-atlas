About

The Stanford NLP Group was started in 2000 by Christopher Manning, who held a joint appointment in linguistics and computer science. It grew to include faculty such as Dan Jurafsky, who arrived in 2004, and Percy Liang, who joined in 2012. The group is part of Stanford University and brings together linguists and computer scientists.

Its software history begins with the Stanford Parser, released as open source in December 2002, and the first unified CoreNLP package appeared on 1 November 2010. Later releases include GloVe word vectors, the Python library Stanza for many human languages, and the DSPy framework for programming rather than prompting language models.

The group publishes code, data and papers openly, and its GitHub organization is among the most widely used sources of NLP tooling for researchers, students and industry engineers.

Mission and ambitions

To advance the computational understanding of human language and to share research software and datasets that others can build on.
