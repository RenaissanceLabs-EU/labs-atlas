About

NVIDIA was founded in 1993 by Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem and is headquartered in Santa Clara, California. It is primarily a hardware and software platform company, and its open-model work sits alongside that business.

Through its Hugging Face organization and GitHub, NVIDIA publishes model checkpoints, large synthetic and curated datasets, and training and inference software. Main families include Nemotron for language and reasoning, Cosmos for physical-AI world models, Isaac GR00T for humanoid robots, and Parakeet, Canary and Magpie for speech.

Research is published as technical reports on arXiv, often with long author lists, and many releases come with training recipes and data. NVIDIA says its Nemotron effort includes open weights, open datasets and published methods.

Mission and ambitions

NVIDIA presents its open releases as a way to let developers and organizations build, customize and deploy AI on its platform, including sovereign and domain-specific systems. It describes Nemotron as open technology covering models, data and recipes.

  2. 2024

  3. 2024

  5. 2025

Licence tags as published on GitHub and Hugging Face, checked 2026-11-17. Open means mostly permissive or copyleft licences; Mixed means a blend; Restricted means mostly custom or non-commercial terms.

Many NVIDIA models ship as open weights, with datasets and code on Hugging Face and GitHub, but licences vary: the NVIDIA Open Model License covers Nemotron-4 and the Nemotron 3 Nano and Super models, newer Nemotron 3 Ultra and 3.5 models are reported under OpenMDW-1.1, NeMo code is Apache-2.0, and speech models range from CC-BY-4.0 (Parakeet) to CC-BY-NC-4.0 (Canary-1B).
