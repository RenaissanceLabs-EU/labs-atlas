About

The German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence was established in 1988 as a non-profit public-private partnership. It began in Kaiserslautern and Saarbrücken, and Wolfgang Wahlster has served as its scientific director since the founding.

Today DFKI operates research facilities in Kaiserslautern, Saarbrücken, Bremen, Oldenburg and Osnabrück, laboratories in Berlin, Darmstadt and Lübeck, and a branch office in Trier. Its work is organized into 29 research departments, thirteen competence centers and eight living labs, and it is funded by the European Union, federal ministries, the German states and the German Research Foundation.

DFKI led the national Verbmobil project on speech translation, which produced several spin-off companies and earned the 2001 German Future Prize. Its public code and datasets appear on GitHub and Hugging Face, where researchers in several departments publish benchmarks and models.

Mission and ambitions

To conduct application-oriented AI research for industry and society and to transfer results into commercial software and spin-offs.

Milestones

  1. 1988

    Founded in Kaiserslautern and Saarbrücken

    DFKI was established as a non-profit public-private partnership for applied AI research.

  2. 2001

    Verbmobil recognised with the German Future Prize

    The national speech-translation project that DFKI led, translating spontaneous German, English and Japanese speech, won the award for its leader's group.

  3. 2017

    Public GitHub organization opened

    DFKI created its GitHub organization to publish research code, later adding the Awesome-DFKI index of its public projects.

  4. 2022

    CGHD handwritten circuit dataset

    DFKI researchers released a public ground-truth dataset of handwritten circuit diagram images.

  5. 2023

    SphereCraft paper repository

    The most-starred repository in the DFKI GitHub organization accompanies the SphereCraft paper.
