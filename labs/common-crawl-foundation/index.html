About

Common Crawl is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit founded in 2007 by Gil Elbaz, who had earlier co-founded Applied Semantics. Its premise was that web-scale crawl data should be available to researchers who lack the resources to run their own crawling infrastructure.

The foundation publishes monthly crawl archives, each typically containing more than two billion pages, in WARC, WAT and WET formats. It also releases URL and CDXJ indexes and host-level and domain-level web graphs. The data is hosted on Amazon Web Services and can be downloaded free of charge.

A small staff of engineers and researchers maintains the crawler and the datasets, supported by a board and an advisory group. The archives serve academic researchers, startups and the teams that build language models.

Mission and ambitions

Common Crawl aims to keep a large, current copy of the public web freely available so that anyone can analyze it. It treats open access to crawl data as a way to widen who can do research on the web and on language technology.
