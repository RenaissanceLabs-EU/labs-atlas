About

The speech recognition group sits in the Department of Information and Communications Engineering at Aalto University in Espoo and is part of the Finnish Center for Artificial Intelligence. It works on acoustic modeling of phonemes, statistical language models for large-vocabulary continuous speech recognition, and speech technology for under-resourced languages.

The group publishes its research software through the aalto-speech GitHub organization, created in 2013. The best known repositories are Morfessor, a tool for unsupervised and semi-supervised morphological segmentation, AaltoASR, a set of recognition tools, and speaker diarization scripts built on that toolkit.

Professor Mikko Kurimo heads the group and is known internationally for unsupervised subword modeling for languages with rich word inflection, such as Finnish, Estonian, Turkish and Arabic. The software mainly serves other speech and language researchers.

Mission and ambitions

The group aims to improve speech recognition with new algorithms and to make recognition practical for languages with limited data and heavy inflection. It shares its tools openly so that other researchers can reuse and extend them.

Milestones

  1. 2013

    Morfessor repository

    The Morfessor tool for unsupervised and semi-supervised morphological segmentation appeared on the group's GitHub organization under a BSD licence.

  2. 2013

    AaltoASR toolkit

    The group published the Aalto Automatic Speech Recognition tools as an open repository.

  3. 2016

    Speaker diarization scripts

    Diarization scripts based on AaltoASR were released and have drawn close to 200 GitHub stars.

  4. 2018

    Subword Kaldi recipes

    A repository showing how to handle position-dependent phones in a subword lexicon FST for Kaldi was published.
